# HEPHAESTUS / Concept exploration record

## Scope and provenance

The project explored thirty concepts for a cinematic, Greek-mythology-inspired JBX Web Studio portfolio experience with a meaningful visitor journey and working backend. The user authorized autonomous selection. This document preserves the supplied concept pool rather than claiming a user interview or validated market research.

The reviewer retained the original six descriptions from the curious-child branch: Sky Has Holes, Gods Lost Property, Persephone Second Garden, Hephaestus One More Thing, Sea Remembers Everything, and Hermes Delivers Impossible. The parent agent also supplied a substantive summary for After Gods and the later sky-repair refinement. For the other twenty-three concepts, only titles were available to this reviewer. Their scores and grouping are explicitly provisional title-level judgments; no missing pitch or original agent evaluation is reconstructed here.

## Thirty-concept pool and scoring

N = novelty, V = viability for a complete portfolio build, F = fit to the cinematic Greek-mythology brief. Each uses a 1–10 judgment scale. Weighted score = N × .35 + V × .40 + F × .25. These are design-selection estimates, not measured outcomes. **D** means a substantive description was available; **T** means title only and therefore provisional. Scores should not be read as evidence that the unavailable pitches were fully evaluated.

## Underlying angles

These clusters expose repeated mechanisms and contrasting opportunities. Membership for title-only concepts is an interpretation of the title, not a recovered description.

- **Divine work made human:** Atlas Off Duty; Weather Treaty; Thirteenth Seat; Ariadne Bureau; Styx Crossing Office; Gods Lost Property. An everyday institution or obligation makes mythology approachable. Trap: administrative jokes can overpower the intended atmosphere, and an interface can become merely a dressed-up form.
- **Passage, routes, and consequences:** Last Ferry; Thirteenth Labor; Ariadne Second Thread; Last Oracle; Hermes Delivers Impossible; Fates Cut Here. The visitor follows a course or makes a consequential choice. Trap: branching narrative volume and unclear puzzle rules can consume the entire build.
- **Repair, care, and cultural memory:** House Forgotten Gods; After Gods; Sky Has Holes; Sea Remembers Everything; Daphne Living Archive. The visitor restores or preserves something threatened. Trap: restoration needs clear before/after continuity and a satisfying visible consequence.
- **Living myth and natural systems:** Persephone Second Garden; Chimera Field Station; Ambrosia Cultures; Hydra Protocol; Nyx Nocturnal Ecologies; Molting Talos. Myth becomes an ecology or evolving organism. Trap: a convincing living system can demand substantial simulation, art, and return-visit logic.
- **Craft, ritual, and a tangible outcome:** Hephaestus One More Thing; Weight Oath; Aeolus Wind Instruments; Demeter Table. The experience centers on making or choosing something with a clear result. Trap: the result must be visibly personal; ornate presentation cannot compensate for arbitrary inputs.
- **Time, absence, and fleeting phenomena:** Chronos Lost Property; Helios One Minute Dawn; Echo Return to Me. A poetic constraint supplies the narrative engine. Trap: the premise may be memorable but lack a complete repeatable product journey.

## Preserved curious-child concepts

1. **Sky Has Holes:** someone stole the stars. Walk across a sleeping Greece, collect fragments of unfinished myths, and restore a living sky. Each constellation opens an illustrated story; the persistent sky becomes a shareable celestial portrait. Later refinement: use astronomical traces and two myth interpretations, then download a personal atlas.
2. **Gods Lost Property:** a mortal runs the office where gods recover Apollo's last sunset, Athena's unanswered question, and Poseidon's favorite wave. Inspect impossible objects, follow clues, and complete returns through a persistent case archive.
3. **Persephone Second Garden:** grow a promise, a farewell, or a remembered summer between the surface and the underworld. Personal planting choices change sculptural plants and persist across visits.
4. **Hephaestus One More Thing:** enter a silent forge and commission an impossible object. Its intention, material, and symbol shape a relic through a tactile sequence, ending with an artifact and its record.
5. **Sea Remembers Everything:** recover memories from submerged rooms to reconstruct a vanished coastal city. Each restored fragment makes the city visibly return above the water.
6. **Hermes Delivers Impossible:** carry messages to places or moments—an apology to yesterday, a warning to a mountain, an invitation to dawn. Fold a vessel, navigate its delivery route, and keep the completed journeys.

## Shortlist and decision

**Chosen: Hephaestus One More Thing.** It has the clearest relationship between cinematic subject matter and functional interaction. Authored geometry can show every choice directly; one compact record can preserve the entire result; downloadable artwork gives the journey a real endpoint. Its constraints support a complete, polished experience without an open-ended story engine or simulated economy.

**Deepened alternative: After Gods.** The restoration premise gives the visitor a morally interesting choice: repair damage or preserve its history. It offers strong editorial imagery and a convincing service structure. Its cost is the requirement for matching damaged/restored assets and the risk of building a larger treatment portal than the portfolio needs.

**Deepened alternative: Sky Has Holes.** Repairing the sky offers an immediately legible transformation and a beautiful personal atlas. Its cost is more difficult spatial interaction, astronomy/story content, and the possibility that a precision puzzle excludes casual phone visitors.

## Three developed sketches

### Hephaestus / One More Thing

Enter a contemporary exhibition inside an ancient forge, encountering the maker before inspecting three original relic forms. Choose an intention, material, and maker's mark at one stable workbench. Each choice changes the rendered object and its authored description. A deliberate forging action closes an orbit around an inscribed core, then releases the finished relic into a quiet presentation. A server validates the recipe and returns a versioned portable record that can be reopened and downloaded as an archival illustrated print. The experience identifies itself as an original fictional JBX Web Studio project.

**Load-bearing risk:** the gallery and hero promise more craft than the live forge can deliver, or the selectable combinations barely differ.

**First builder step:** finish one representative form with two convincing material states, one visible mark, a server-created recipe, a matching print, and a reopened share page before expanding the scenes.

**Useful variations:** ceremonial shields; objects for impossible tasks; sculptural vow rings; objects addressing human limitations. These are alternative directions, not additional launch features.

### After the Gods / Restoration Atelier

Divine catastrophes have left behind damaged objects: a bronze lyre, a wing, a crown. Enter an atelier that treats each object as a physical witness. Inspect its fractures, wear, and surviving detail through a guided forensic view. Choose whether the treatment preserves its scars or restores a selected element. See a matched treatment result and keep a record of the chosen approach. The emotional endpoint is custodianship: the visitor has decided which history should remain visible.

**Load-bearing risk:** inconsistent before/after images make the central restoration feel false; extensive commissions and treatment management can turn the concept into an unnecessarily large service portal.

**First builder step:** create one exact damaged/restored pair with a working comparison view and two clearly explained preservation choices, then generate a treatment record from that selection.

### The Sky Has Holes / A Personal Atlas

Begin under a night sky with missing constellations. Follow astronomical traces to understand where a lost pattern belongs, then encounter two interpretations of its myth. Restore the pattern with a forgiving interaction and select the interpretation that accompanies it. The repaired sky visibly accumulates the visitor's choices. A saved configuration reopens that same sky, while a downloadable atlas records its restored figures and selected stories. The ending is a sky the visitor helped author.

**Load-bearing risk:** precise positioning becomes a frustrating game, particularly on phones; astronomical claims and alternate myth content require careful sourcing.

**First builder step:** build one constellation with generous snapping, a keyboard-equivalent restore action, two concise sourced story options, and one atlas page generated from the chosen interpretation.

## Wildcard

**Gods Lost Property** remains the strongest tonal departure among the fully described concepts. Its impossible objects allow unexpected art direction and humane writing without requiring a combat or quest format. A single complete case—inspect, connect clues, identify the owner, return the object—would test whether it can stay sophisticated rather than becoming a novelty dashboard. It is preserved as an alternative, not added to the forge's scope.

## Resolved decisions and boundaries

- The chosen experience is a fictional digital atelier, with no pretend sales, fabricated customers, NFT ownership, or archaeological authenticity claims.
- Its “provenance” is a fictional digital record of a recipe. A checksum detects accidental corruption; it is not an authenticity or security signature.
- Curated intentions and marks replace free-text submissions. Accounts, private messaging, a public gallery, multiplayer, and game progression are not necessary to complete the selected journey.
- A versioned self-contained share URL is the portable record; a local collection is explicitly a convenience on the current device. This is not remote database persistence.
- The launch goal is a complete forge-to-record-to-download experience with strong visual continuity. Optional social features, deeper games, and alternative concept mechanics are not release blockers.
