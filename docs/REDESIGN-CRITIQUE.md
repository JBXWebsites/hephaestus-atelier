# HEPHAESTUS / Spatial redesign critique

Reviewed the public site at `https://hephaestus-atelier.vercel.app` on 2026-10-30 at 1440 × 1000 and 390 × 844. Inspected the hero, maker, collection, invocation, and loaded forge. Used reduced motion for stable structural captures; motion observations also draw on the existing implementation and the preceding normal-motion public verification. Loading placeholders and fixed-header artifacts from initial element captures were excluded from design findings. No app files were changed.

Applied Impeccable's brand design guidance after loading the current PRODUCT.md and DESIGN.md. This is the requested independent design critique, not an invocation of the skill's separate scored audit or detector workflow. The main task owns the deeper 21st.dev component research; this document does not claim to have inspected that catalog.

## Judgment

The site is functional and visually consistent, but its composition feels assembled from premium landing-page sections. Its distinguishing idea, a visitor shaping a divine object, becomes visible too late and occupies a conventional preview-plus-form layout. The current solution substitutes monumental type, expansive padding, and dramatic images for a spatial relationship between scenes. Adding more parallax to these same rectangles would preserve that problem.

This is a judgment about the rendered design. It is not evidence that a particular model produced the site, that visitors can reliably identify AI authorship, or that the implementation lacks effort. Passing accessibility and functional checks also does not establish distinction or satisfy the user's stated aesthetic goal.

Keep three valuable foundations: the existing artwork's consistent mineral/fire lighting, the original usable relic configurator, and the restrained color/material family. Redesign how they meet.

### Component-level consequences

- **Navigation:** the tall horizontal header with ruled CTA reinforces a standard luxury-site frame. Keep navigation legible but reduce its visual claim. A compact maker's emblem, two text destinations, and one clear forge action are sufficient. A large branded bar should not become the page's strongest persistent shape.
- **Workbench choices:** three rows of equal rectangular tiles read as a generic settings form. Keep their native semantics, but differentiate the task: a typographic intention selection; visibly material swatches with names; large recognizable mark buttons. Use one shared alignment and selection language so the distinction is purposeful rather than three unrelated component styles.
- **Object descriptions:** the gallery's ruled index is serviceable. Its problem is the amount of surrounding preamble and the absence of the active object while making choices on mobile, not that lists or rules are inherently generic.
- **Spacing:** specify relationships instead of one reusable section padding. Start with 12–20px within a label/value unit, 24–40px between related content, 56–88px between scene beats, and a single larger pause where the visitor changes from looking to making. These are starting proportions, not a replacement mechanical template.
- **Finish:** the result should appear in the same physical location as the configured object, with download/share actions revealed beside it. Avoid a second disconnected success panel that feels bolted onto a form.

## Direction A: The continuous foundry

**Recommended for the user's request for layering, parallax, and a natural spatial rhythm.** The visitor moves from the maker's monumental presence, through the workshop threshold, to one object on an illuminated bench. The visual continuity comes from the atelier's architecture and the object itself.

1. **Threshold:** retain the existing Hephaestus artwork, but compose the heading as an inscription attached to a quiet foreground plane. Let one workshop crop become visible at the lower boundary before the hero completely leaves. Preserve readable text over stable contrast; do not place critical words behind the statue's face.
2. **Workshop:** replace the pale, evenly divided maker section with a taller architectural crop occupying roughly two thirds of the width and a shorter offset reading surface. A compact paragraph identifies Hephaestus and explains the imagined atelier. Its baseline need not align with the image's top edge. The next object enters over the image boundary.
3. **Objects become the bench:** introduce the actual live relic as the visitor encounters the three intentions. Keep the object as the same dominant shape as the controls arrive. The gallery's photographic studies become optional smaller sketches, using existing images. Remove the separate 843px invocation scene; a restrained ember field and a short inscription can mark the transition.
4. **Making:** compress the heading above the workbench. Use a broad object field and a narrow, clearly grouped choice rail. The object is free of an enclosing card; the controls remain still while its material and mark change.
5. **Possession:** the sealing motion ends where the result will live. The object settles, its identifier and inscription become readable, and the actual download/share actions arrive within the same composition.

**Motion rule:** two or three depth planes with a consistent direction, not independent random drifting. Start with roughly 20–64px of differential movement between image/architecture and foreground text at desktop, and less on phones. Use scroll to reveal the relationship between planes. Avoid mandatory long pins; preserve immediate forge access and a static, equally clear reduced-motion composition. GSAP remains responsible for authored motion.

**Mobile composition:** keep the hero crop, shorten the maker passage, preserve one controlled overlap, and prioritize compact preview/control co-visibility. The desktop overlap cannot simply become three oversized stacked blocks. Use the existing artwork and geometry; no new generated assets are required.

**Main risk:** excessive overlapping layers can damage reading order and become a collage of unrelated floating items. Limit the motif to architectural planes and the relic. No ornamental coordinates, generic glass panels, cursor effects, or particle field is needed.

## Direction B: The maker's bench

**A distinct alternative with a shorter path to agency.** The site opens around an inspectable relic and the invitation to shape it. Hephaestus and the workshop supply the surrounding story instead of occupying several introductory screens.

1. **Object-first opening:** give the actual live relic the central or deliberately off-center stage, with a compact inscription on one side and three intention choices on the other. The existing statue image appears as a cropped vertical presence or a low-contrast backdrop outside the functional reading areas. The visitor understands the making promise before scrolling.
2. **A wide working surface:** selecting an intention changes the same object. Material samples appear as a tactile row below its presentation; marks sit beside the object's face with clear labels. Keep controls visually grounded in the bench, without mimicking a game HUD.
3. **Story at the edge:** move the maker story into a lower architectural spread with one substantial workshop image and an accessible myth disclosure. Visitors can read it before or after creating. Existing collection studies form a small, varied archive rather than three identical product cards.
4. **The record as an edition sheet:** the completed relic and its print share the same scale and typographic signature. The page resolves into one useful keepsake, not a final generic inspirational slogan.

**Rhythm:** immediate high density around the object, one generous workshop interlude, then a compact closing. Typography uses Marcellus for the maker's name, relic names, and inscriptions; the interface's functional hierarchy receives enough size and weight to stand beside it. Ember appears primarily when heating/sealing and in selected control states.

**Motion rule:** prioritize object inspection, material response, and precise assembly. Ambient parallax is restrained because the bench already supplies spatial depth. A slight movement relationship between the backdrop and the object is sufficient. Mobile keeps a compact preview and nearby controls rather than reproducing the desktop spread as a tall column.

**Main risk:** putting all choices in the opening can overwhelm a first-time visitor. Default to one finished-looking object, three labeled intentions, and a single next action; reveal the remaining choice groups in-place with direct access. Avoid a mandatory introductory overlay.

## Build and review order

1. Prototype one continuous **maker → object → controls** composition at 1440px and 390px using the existing assets and live relic. Resolve the mobile preview/control relationship first.
2. Choose Direction A or B as the governing composition. Use the main task's 21st.dev findings for specific mechanisms, such as layered image framing, restrained transitions, or selection behavior. Do not import a catalog demo's whole visual identity or combine several unrelated showcase effects.
3. Set the revised type and spacing hierarchy, remove redundant labels/slogans, and bring the object interaction forward. Inspect the page with motion off before choreographing it.
4. Add one shared depth grammar and one sealing culmination. Review scene boundaries and intermediate frames, not only each section's ideal screenshot.
5. Verify that selection, preview, final record, share link, and print remain consistent; preserve keyboard, reduced motion, no-JavaScript reading, and the already working backend.

**Acceptance evidence:** a visitor can identify what to make and how to start in the first screen; mobile material/mark changes occur while the preview is visible; at least two major transitions share a deliberate visual element; no sequence of three major scenes repeats the same label/heading/split structure; useful prose is comfortably readable; and the final artifact retains the same visual identity as the chosen live object. These are review criteria, not a promise that a checklist alone produces distinctive design.
